# Transfer Risk Assessment

**Internal — Your Business Today Ltd — Version 1.0 — 17 November 2026**

The documented transfer risk assessment promised in section 4.2 of Schedule 1 (Data Processing Agreement), for personal data in client Solutions and in YBT's Platform that is reached from outside the United Kingdom. YBT starts these transfers, so the assessment is YBT's. It follows the steps in the Information Commissioner's transfer risk assessment tool. Review it when a sub-processor is added or changed, when the law changes, and at least once a year.

## 2. The transfer mechanism

Each recipient's data processing terms include the UK Addendum to the EU Standard Contractual Clauses, approved under section 119A of the Data Protection Act 2018. Hosting in the European Economic Area needs none, since the UK's adequacy regulations cover it.

## 3. How much harm a transfer could do

The data is ordinary business data: names, work contact details, job titles and records of work. Clients may not put special category data into a Solution unless their Order Form allows it (Schedule 3). If such data were reached by a public authority abroad, the likely harm to the people in it is low: it would expose work contact details and business records, not health, money or anything that would put a person at risk. If a client's Order Form allows special category data, this assessment is repeated for that client before any is held.

## 4. The law and practice where the data goes

The United States is where most of the routine transfers go. Its surveillance laws can reach data held by these providers. The risk of them being used against a client's ordinary business records is low, and people in the UK can use the redress mechanism under Executive Order 14086, which has applied to the UK since the UK Extension to the EU-US Data Privacy Framework came into force in October 2023. A recipient certified to that Extension is also covered by UK adequacy regulations. [Record here, for each recipient, whether it is certified and what its terms say about government requests for customer data.]

## 5. Extra protections

- Encryption in transit and at rest, backups included.
- Access for named people only, with multi-factor authentication, and no more access in an AI session than the person using it has.
- Support access only when a support case needs it.
- AI sessions on one client at a time.
- The Solution and the Platform send nothing to an AI model themselves.

## 6. Conclusion

With the UK Addendum and the protections in section 5, the transfers give the people in the data protection that is not materially lower than in the United Kingdom. They may continue.
