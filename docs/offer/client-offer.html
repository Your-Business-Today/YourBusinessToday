The seven steps

From the first conversation to long after launch.

  1. 01

    Map your data sources

    Week one · with the people who do the work

    We sit with the people who do the work and map everything that comes into your business and everything that goes out: the quote, the order, the job sheet, the invoice, the photograph that proves the work was done, and the processes that turn one into the next.

    You get: a written map of your business in plain English, and a fixed price for the build. Yours to keep whether or not you go further.

  2. 02

    Build your data harness

    Weeks one and two

    The harness is one secure place for everything the map found. Every fact about your business has its place in it, every value is checked before it goes in, and every person sees only what their role allows.

    You get: your data in one place, backed up, with the first spreadsheets and documents retired into it.

  3. 03

    Your web app

    Weeks two to four

    Screens over the harness, built around how your team really works: the jobs at the stage they are really at, the invoices that are due, the decisions that need the owner. No training course, because it works the way you already do.

    You get: your own app, live and in use, at your own web address. New screens are asked for in a chat and built without touching the data underneath.

  4. 04

    Your AI connector

    Live with the web app

    Your AI assistant, Claude or ChatGPT, is connected to the same system. Ask what is in flight, raise a job, chase an invoice or run the month end from a chat, and it does exactly what the app does under exactly the same permissions.

    You get: everyone on your team with an assistant that knows your business, and can do nothing through it that they could not do on the screen.

  1. 05

    Skills for your assistant

    From week three · one process at a time

    Your processes are written down as skills your assistant follows from start to finish: the valuation put together every month, the report written from the week on site, the invoice that has to be chased. The judgement stays with your people and the repetitive work goes to the assistant.

    You get: a growing set of skills in your own words. Change one and it is in force from your next conversation; add one whenever the business changes.

  2. 06

    Run it in the open

    From the first conversation

    Your business gets a project in our project management system on day one. Every goal and task has its own conversation, you see what is being worked on and what comes next, and your own assistant can read the project and raise work on it.

    You get: nothing hidden and nothing dropped. You always know where the build is, and you can ask for a change from anywhere.

  3. 07

    Change it however you want

    Monthly retainer · one monthly fee · cancel on a month’s notice

    Most businesses keep us on, because a business never stops changing. A new screen, a new process for the assistant, a new record the business now needs: ask for it in a chat and watch it being built. Hosting, backups and support are all inside the same fee.

    You get: your app kept running, kept current and kept yours. The harness, the app and the skills belong to you, and they leave with you if you ever leave us.

Case study

A family run construction company

Loft conversions, extensions and whole house renovations across the South of England, with sixty five years of trade experience in the family.

The building was never the problem. It was everything around it. The day on site arrived in the office as messages and photographs. Somebody then had to turn them into a report for the client. Drawings came back marked up. Valuations were put together by hand. The person who knew where everything was could never take a Friday off.

We spent a week on paper with the people who do the work. Then we spent a week proving the idea in code. Everything arriving at the business is a communication and a person makes one decision about it. After that the harness groups them and the assistant writes what has to be written.

The company now runs on a portal that shows every project at the stage it is really at. A plain English request becomes a build they can watch. Their own AI assistant can read a job or raise a request without opening the site.
